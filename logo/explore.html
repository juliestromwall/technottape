Tech Not Tape — six logo directions

Each shown as the mark alone, the horizontal lockup you'd put in the site header, and reversed on charcoal. The small row is a real 24 px and 32 px favicon test — a logo that dies at that size is not a logo.

Direction A
The Stack
Three bars, squared off and neatly aligned — the opposite of a crossed strip of tape. Reads as things stacked properly. This is the placeholder mark, refined.

Direction B
tnt.
A lowercase monogram with a full stop — the same device as the js. mark on your business card, so the personal and business brands are visibly siblings. TNT also happens to mean "not gentle about fixing things".

Direction C
Not
No symbol at all — the name is the idea, so set it and colour the word that carries the meaning. Three tight lines echo the three services. Cheapest to reproduce anywhere and impossible to draw badly.

Direction D
The Joint
Two blocks interlocked like a dovetail — a joint that holds because of how it is cut, not because of what is stuck on it. The most literal expression of "built properly", and it borrows nothing from tech clichés.

Patched, then Solid
